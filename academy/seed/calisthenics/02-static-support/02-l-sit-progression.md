The L-sit — supported on your hands, legs straight out in front — builds brutal core
compression and straight-arm strength, and it's the gateway to the V-sit and manna.

## The progression

1. **Foot-supported L-sit** — on parallettes, heels on the floor, press down hard. 3×20 s.
2. **Tuck L-sit** — knees tucked to chest, hips fully off the floor. *Target: 3×20 s.*
3. **One-leg-extended L-sit** — alternate legs. 3×15 s each.
4. **Full L-sit** — both legs straight, parallel to the floor. *Target: 3×15 s.*
5. **Raised / advanced** — L-sit on the floor (harder — less clearance), then work toward the V-sit.

## Form cues

- **Depress and protract** the shoulders — the same "push the floor away" as the support hold.
- **Actively pull the toes up** and squeeze the quads; compression comes from the hip flexors *and* the abs.
- **Posterior pelvic tilt** — round the low back slightly to bring the legs up, don't just lift with the hip.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Legs drift below parallel | Regress to tuck; build compression with seated leg lifts |
| Shoulders shrug | Cue depression first, then lift the legs |
| Holding breath | Breathe shallow but continuously |

## Accessory work

Seated compression lifts (sit on floor, legs straight, lift heels off ground) and
banded leg raises. 3×10, twice a week.
