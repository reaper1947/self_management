Everyone understands training hard. Almost nobody plans the weeks where they do
not, which is why almost everybody stalls.

## What a deload is

A planned week of reduced volume that lets accumulated fatigue drain so
adaptation can show up. It is not a week off and it is not a reward.

The mechanism is simple. Fitness and fatigue both rise when you train. What you
can actually *do* is fitness minus fatigue. Cut the training, fatigue falls faster
than fitness, and performance goes **up**.

That is why people come back from a deload stronger. Nothing was gained during the
easy week. It was gained weeks earlier and was hidden under fatigue.

## When

**Every fourth week**, planned in advance. Or sooner, if two or more of these are
true:

- performance down for more than a week
- resting heart rate up several beats
- sleep worse than usual
- joints achy in the morning
- motivation gone
- irritable with people who do not deserve it

The last two are real signals, not character flaws.

## How

**Halve your sets. Keep the movements and the intensity.**

- 4 sets of 8 push-ups → 2 sets of 8
- 5 sets of 15 s tuck lever → 2 or 3 sets of 15 s
- keep joint prep exactly as it is
- keep handstand practice — it is skill, not load

Do **not** turn a deload week into a "different training" week. A week of running
instead of a week of levers is not a deload; it is the same fatigue wearing a
different hat.

## Peaking for a test

If you want a maximum — a first muscle-up, a heaviest weighted pull-up, a best
front lever hold — plan three weeks back:

- **Week −3:** normal training
- **Week −2:** volume down 30%, intensity the same
- **Week −1:** volume down 60%, one or two sharp sessions only
- **Test day:** fresh, warm, no new variables

Most people test at the end of a hard block, tired, and conclude they made no
progress. They made the progress. They tested it under a pile of fatigue.

## Testing properly

- same time of day, same warm-up, same equipment
- rested — two easy days beforehand
- one attempt at the maximum, not five
- film it
- write it down

**Every six weeks.** More often is measuring noise.

## Long-term structure

A sensible year:

| Block | Length | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Accumulation | 3 weeks | volume, adding sets and seconds |
| Deload | 1 week | half volume |
| Intensification | 3 weeks | harder rungs, lower volume |
| Deload | 1 week | half volume |
| Test | 1 week | peak and measure |

Nine weeks. Four cycles fits a year with a fortnight spare for life.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Deloads make me lose progress" | did nothing at all instead of half | keep the movements, halve the sets |
| Never deloading | feeling fine | the week you feel you need one, you needed it a fortnight ago |
| Testing every session | impatience | six weeks; write the date down |
| Progress stalls every 6–8 weeks | no deload in the plan | add one every fourth week |
