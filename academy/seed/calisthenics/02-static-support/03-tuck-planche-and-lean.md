The planche — body held horizontal, supported only by the hands, no feet — is the peak
static push skill. The road there is the **planche lean** and the **tuck planche**.

## The progression

1. **Planche lean** — in a push-up plank on parallettes, shift your shoulders forward past
   your hands, keeping arms straight and scapulae protracted. The further forward, the
   harder. *Target: 3×20 s at a lean where your shoulders are well ahead of your wrists.*
2. **Frog stand / crow** — knees on elbows, balance. Builds the wrist and straight-arm base. 3×30 s.
3. **Tuck planche** — knees tucked tight to chest, hips at shoulder height, **arms straight**,
   back rounded (protracted). *Target: 3×15 s.*
4. **Advanced tuck planche** — hips open, back flat, knees still bent. 3×10 s.
5. **Straddle planche** → **full planche** — years of work; covered in the advanced lessons.

## Form cues

- **Arms dead straight.** A bent-arm "planche" is a different (easier) skill and stalls progress.
- **Protract hard** — push the upper back to the ceiling, round it.
- **Posterior pelvic tilt**, glutes on, point the toes.
- **Lean is everything.** Planche strength = how far forward your shoulders can go past your hands.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Elbows bend under load | Reduce lean / regress to lean holds only |
| Hips sag below shoulders | Push harder into protraction; think "pike up" |
| Wrist pain | More wrist prep; use parallettes; build frog stand time |

## Programming

Planche work is high-intensity, low-volume. 2 sessions/week, 4–6 sets of 5–15 s holds,
full rest between. Progress by leaning 1–2 cm further or adding 2–3 s.

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×20 s planche lean, shoulders clearly past the wrists, arms straight
- [ ] 3×15 s tuck planche, arms straight, back rounded
- [ ] No elbow or wrist pain 24 h after a session
