The dip is the deadlift of upper-body pushing — big loads on the chest, triceps and
shoulders. It's also the most-injured calisthenics movement, so the ramp matters.

## The progression

1. **Support hold** (from the static track) — own 3×30 s before you dip.
2. **Negative dips** — jump to the top, lower over 3–5 s to shoulders-below-elbows. 4×3.
3. **Band- or foot-assisted dips** — least assistance for 5 clean reps; reduce weekly.
4. **Full dips.** *Target: 3×8 to full depth.*
5. **Weighted dips** — add load once 3×10 bodyweight is easy. Progress ~2.5 kg/2 weeks.
6. **Ring dips** (turned out) — a big step up in stability demand.

## Form cues

- **Depth:** shoulders below the elbows — but no deeper than pain-free, especially early.
- **Slight forward lean** for more chest, upright for more triceps. Pick one and be consistent.
- **Shoulders down and back** at the bottom — never let them shrug up to the ears.
- Elbows track back, not flared wide.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Shoulders shrug at the bottom | Regress; drill the support hold + scapular depression |
| Bouncing out of the bottom | Pause 1 s at depth; slow the descent |
| Chasing depth before strength | Half-depth strict beats full-depth sloppy |
| Front-of-shoulder pain | Reduce depth and lean; more warm-up; deload |

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×8 full-depth strict dips, shoulders stable
- [ ] 4×3 slow (4 s) negatives to full depth
- [ ] No shoulder pain the day after
