Two movements that point in different directions: the pike push-up leads to the
handstand push-up, and the pseudo-planche push-up leads to the planche. Both are
where your pushing strength stops being about the chest and starts being about
the shoulders.

## The pike push-up

### What it is

Hands and feet on the floor, hips high, body in an inverted V, head descending
toward the floor between your hands.

### The physics

The steeper your hips, the closer your torso comes to vertical, and the more of
your weight lands over your hands rather than being shared with your feet. A flat
pike is roughly a shoulder-biased push-up. A near-vertical pike with the feet
elevated is approaching a handstand push-up.

It is, again, a continuous dial: **the angle of your torso is the progression.**

### The progression

| Rung | What to do | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pike push-up, feet on floor** | 3×8 |
| 2 | **Feet elevated 30 cm** | 3×8 |
| 3 | **Feet elevated 60 cm** | 3×8 |
| 4 | **Deficit pike push-up** — hands on blocks | 3×5 |
| 5 | **Wall handstand push-up, partial** | 3×3 |
| 6 | **Wall handstand push-up, full range** | 3×5 |

### Form cues

**Head goes forward of the hands, not between them.** Aim the top of your head
just in front of your fingertips; it keeps the shoulder in a safer path.

**Elbows at about 45°.** Same rule as a push-up.

**Keep the hips stacked.** As you fatigue the hips drop and it becomes a
decline push-up — easier, and no longer the exercise.

## The pseudo-planche push-up

### What it is

A push-up with your hands beside your waist rather than under your shoulders, and
your shoulders leaning forward past your hands throughout.

### The physics

This is the planche lean with a push-up added. Your centre of mass sits behind
your hands, so your shoulders carry real torque — and unlike the planche lean,
that torque is present while you are also pressing.

It is the most direct bent-arm assistance exercise for the planche that exists,
and it is significantly harder than a normal push-up at the same bodyweight.

### The progression

| Rung | What to do | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Planche lean hold** | 30 s |
| 2 | **PPP, hands at ribs, small lean** | 3×5 |
| 3 | **PPP, hands at waist** | 3×8 |
| 4 | **PPP, feet elevated** | 3×5 |
| 5 | **PPP on parallettes, deep** | 3×5 |

### Form cues

**Lean the whole time.** The shoulders stay in front of the hands at the bottom
*and* the top. If you drift back to a normal push-up position at the top, you have
done a normal push-up.

**Protract and tuck the pelvis.** Same shape as the planche lean.

**Fingers turned slightly out** if your wrists complain. Parallettes solve it
entirely.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pike: hips drop mid-set | fatigue | end the set; the reps after that are a different exercise |
| Pike: shoulder pinch | head going straight down | aim the head forward of the hands |
| PPP: shoulders drift back | avoiding the lean | mark hand position, film it |
| PPP: elbow ache | this is straight-arm-adjacent load | count it toward your straight-arm budget |
| Wrists complain in both | wrist extension | parallettes |

## Programming it

Twice a week, one as a main lift and one as accessory. Pike work pairs naturally
with handstand practice; PPP pairs with planche lean and should be counted in the
same weekly volume.

## Target

**3×8 pike push-ups with feet elevated 60 cm**, and **3×8 pseudo-planche push-ups
with hands at the waist.**

## Self-check

- [ ] Both movements filmed once from the side
- [ ] Pike hips stay stacked through the whole set
- [ ] PPP shoulders stay in front of the hands at lockout
- [ ] Elbows and wrists quiet the following day
