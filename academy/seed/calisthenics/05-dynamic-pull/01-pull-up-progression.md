The pull-up is the best single test of relative upper-body strength — and almost nobody
teaches it as a progression. This is the exact ladder from "I can't do one" to clean reps.

## The standard

- Start from a **full dead hang** — arms straight, shoulders active.
- **Chin clears the bar** with no kip, swing or knee jerk.
- **Lower under control** to a full hang.
- Grip shoulder-width, palms away (pronated).

## The progression

1. **Dead hang** — *Target: 45 s active hang.*
2. **Scapular pulls** — tiny pull-ups from the shoulder blades, arms straight. *3×8 slow.*
3. **Negatives** — jump/step to the top, lower over a genuine 5-count. *5×1 at 5 s.*
4. **Band- or foot-assisted pull-ups** — least help for 5 clean reps; reduce weekly.
5. **Full pull-ups** — *3×5 strict*, then build to 3×8–10.
6. **Weighted / archer pulls** — next lesson.

## Form cues

- **"Bend the bar"** — even with straight arms, try to snap it. Fires the lats.
- **Lead with the chest**, not the chin.
- **Control the eccentric** — falling the last few inches trains nothing.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Can't feel the back | Weeks of scapular pulls |
| Negatives "fall" then slow at the end | Control the first inches out of the top |
| Kipping past the sticking point | Use assistance instead — kipping hides the weakness |
| Half reps | Full range or reset the rep |

## Self-check

- [ ] 45 s active dead hang
- [ ] I can feel my lats during scapular pulls
- [ ] 5 negatives that each take a real 5 seconds
- [ ] Assistance reduced to near-zero
