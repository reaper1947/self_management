The dip is the strongest pushing pattern available to you without equipment, and
the one with the worst safety reputation — almost entirely because of how people
do them, not because of the movement.

## What it is

Supported on parallel bars, arms locked. Lower until your upper arm is roughly
parallel to the ground, then press back to lockout.

## The physics

![Upright versus leaning dip](/academy/figures/dip-lean.svg)

Your hands are the pivot. What changes everything is **where your torso is
relative to them**.

**Upright** — your centre of mass hangs almost directly below your hands, so d is
close to zero and there is very little torque about the shoulder. The work is at
the elbow, and this is a triceps movement.

**Leaning forward** — your chest travels in front of your hands and a real lever
arm opens up. Now the shoulder must produce torque to hold you, and the chest and
front delt take over.

Both are correct dips. They are just different exercises, and the reason to know
which one you are doing is that progressing both at once means progressing
neither.

## Depth, honestly

The often-repeated claim that dips damage shoulders is mostly about **depth**.
Going far below upper-arm-parallel puts the shoulder into deep extension under
heavy load — the same position the german hang trains gently, except now with your
entire bodyweight and a bent arm.

Upper arm parallel to the floor is plenty for almost everybody. Going deeper is a
choice that should be made deliberately, earned slowly, and skipped entirely if
you have any shoulder history.

## The progression

| Rung | What to do | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Support hold** | 30 s |
| 2 | **Negative dip** — 5 s lower, step back up | 3×5 |
| 3 | **Band-assisted dip** | 3×8 |
| 4 | **Feet-assisted dip** — bench or box behind | 3×8 |
| 5 | **Full dip** | 3×5 |
| 6 | **Full dip** | 3×10 |
| 7 | **Paused dip** — 2 s at the bottom | 3×8 |
| 8 | **Weighted dip** | 3×5 at +10 kg |
| 9 | **Ring dip** | 3×5 |
| 10 | **Ring dip, turned out at the top** | 3×5 |

Rung 9 is a large jump. Rings add rotational instability, and the shoulder has to
stabilise in a plane that bars remove entirely. Own 3×10 clean bar dips first.

## Form cues

**Shoulders down, always.** Even at the bottom. Shrugging at the bottom is the
single most common cause of dip shoulder pain.

**Pick your lean and keep it.** Decide upright or leaning for the whole block.

**Elbows back, not out.** They should travel behind you, not flare sideways.

**Control the bottom.** No bouncing. The bottom is where the shoulder is most
vulnerable and where the stretch reflex tempts you.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Front-of-shoulder pain | too deep, or shrugging at the bottom | stop at parallel; press the shoulders down |
| Elbow pain | volume or flare | reduce sets, tuck the elbows |
| Cannot get out of the bottom | weak at long muscle length | paused dips at a lower rung, plus negatives |
| Swinging | no brace | hollow body, legs together, glutes on |
| Bars too wide | fixed equipment | find narrower bars — wide bars flare the elbows for you |

## Programming it

Twice a week for the main progression, 3–5 sets. Move rungs when you can do
**3×10 clean** at the current one.

Dips and push-ups on the same day is fine. Dips and heavy planche work on the same
day is not — both are heavy shoulder-flexion load and it adds up faster than you
would think.

## Target

**3 sets of 10 clean dips to upper-arm parallel, shoulders down throughout.**

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×10 dips at a consistent depth and lean
- [ ] Shoulders stay depressed even at the bottom
- [ ] No bouncing out of the bottom
- [ ] Shoulders and elbows quiet the next day
