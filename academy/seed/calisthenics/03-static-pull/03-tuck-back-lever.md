The back lever — body horizontal, face down, hanging from a bar behind you with straight
arms — is the first "impressive" straight-arm pull skill. Build it from the german hang.

## The progression

1. **German hang hold** — the prerequisite. 3×30 s straight-body (previous lesson).
2. **Tuck back lever** — from an inverted hang, lower until your back is horizontal, knees
   tucked tight, **arms straight**. *Target: 3×15 s.*
3. **Advanced tuck back lever** — open the hips so the thighs are in line with the torso,
   knees still bent. 3×10 s.
4. **Straddle back lever** — legs wide and straight. 3×8 s.
5. **Full back lever** — legs together and straight. The standard.

## Form cues

- **Arms locked.** Check with a partner or video — elbows love to sneak into a bend.
- **Protract and depress** the shoulders; think about pulling the bar toward your hips.
- **Posterior pelvic tilt**, glutes and legs tight — no arching.
- Grip: start **supinated** (palms up) which is stronger for beginners, progress to pronated.

## Programming

2×/week, 4–5 sets of 5–15 s holds, long rest. Also train the **eccentric**: from inverted
hang, lower to horizontal over 5 s, 3×3. Progress by opening the tuck or adding seconds.

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×15 s solid tuck back lever, arms verified straight
- [ ] 3×3 slow (5 s) lowers to horizontal
- [ ] Can hold advanced tuck (hips open) for 8 s
