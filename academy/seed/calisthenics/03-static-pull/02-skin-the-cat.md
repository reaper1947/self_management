Skin the cat is the german hang turned into a movement. It is the first time you
travel through a full rotation under your own control, and it is the bridge from
"I can hang" to "I can control my body in space while hanging."

## What it is

From a hang: pull your knees to your chest, rotate backward through the bar until
you are in a german hang, then reverse it and come back up.

## The physics

Two ideas, and one of them is new to the course.

**Moment of inertia.** Rotating your body about the bar is easier when your mass
is close to the axis. Moment of inertia scales with the *square* of distance
(`I = Σ m r²`), so folding into a tuck does not halve the difficulty of rotating
— it does far more than that. This is why the tuck version is not slightly easier
than the straight-leg version, it is dramatically easier.

Note carefully how this differs from the static lever rule. In a **hold**, what
matters is d, and it enters linearly. In a **rotation**, what matters is r², and it
enters quadratically. Same tuck, two different mechanisms, two different sizes of
benefit.

**Torque at the bottom.** As you pass into the german hang, your centre of mass
swings away from the bar and the load on your shoulders peaks — at exactly the
point where they are in their most vulnerable position. That is why control
through the bottom matters more than anything else here.

## The progression

| Rung | What to do | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **German hang** — prerequisite | 30 s |
| 2 | **Tuck hang** — knees to chest, hold | 20 s |
| 3 | **Half skin the cat** — rotate to horizontal, return | 5 controlled reps |
| 4 | **Tuck skin the cat** — full rotation, tucked | 5 controlled reps |
| 5 | **Skin the cat, one leg extended** | 5 each side |
| 6 | **Straight-leg skin the cat** | 5 reps |
| 7 | **Skin the cat on rings** | 5 reps |

## Form cues

**Slow.** Three seconds down, three seconds back. If you are dropping into the
bottom, you are not training this, you are gambling with it.

**Straight arms throughout.** Bending the elbow turns it into a different
movement and removes the reason to do it.

**Lead with the hips, not the feet.** Think about sending your hips backward
through the gap rather than throwing your feet over.

**Stop before your range ends.** You should finish each rep able to do another.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dropping through the bottom | too little control for the rung | go back to half skin the cat |
| Shoulder pain at the bottom | insufficient extension range | back to the german hang for two weeks |
| Cannot get back up | usually the tuck opening early | stay tucked until you have cleared the bar |
| Grip fails first | expected on rings; normal | use a bar for now, add hangs |
| Feeling dizzy | inner ear, normal early | fewer reps, more sets, it adapts fast |

## Programming it

Twice a week, 3–5 sets of 3–5 slow reps. Counts toward your straight-arm weekly
budget — at roughly 6 seconds per rep, a 4×5 session is about 120 seconds.

Never do this tired. It is a control exercise, and control is the first thing
fatigue removes.

## Target

**5 slow, controlled tuck skin the cats with a 3-second descent.** Then the tuck
back lever.
