This module is the pulling half of the static track, and it starts with a
position rather than a skill — because everything that follows requires your
shoulders to go somewhere most people's shoulders have never been.

## What it is

Hanging from a bar or rings, arms straight, rotating backward until your body
hangs on the far side and your shoulders are in **deep extension** — arms behind
your torso, chest facing away.

## Why it comes first

Skin the cat, the back lever and every inverted transition pass through this
position. If your shoulders lack the range, you do not skip the position — your
body finds it anyway, under load, with no control. That is precisely how people
hurt themselves.

The german hang is where you earn that range slowly and deliberately, with almost
no torque involved.

## The physics, and the specific risk

In extension, the head of the humerus sits forward in the joint and the front of
the capsule — along with the long head of the biceps — takes tension. Add load in
that position and you are stressing tissue in the shallowest, least supported part
of its range.

That is not an argument against training it. Range you never load is range you do
not really have. It is an argument for **doing it slowly, in control, with your
own bodyweight and nothing else**, and for never, ever dropping into it.

Falling into a german hang from the top is the single most common shoulder injury
in calisthenics.

## The progression

| Rung | What to do | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shoulder extension stretch** — hands behind on a low bar, sink slowly | 30 s |
| 2 | **Bar hang with scapular pulls** | 30 s hang, 10 pulls |
| 3 | **Feet-supported german hang** — low bar, feet on the floor taking weight | 30 s |
| 4 | **Assisted german hang** — toes lightly on the ground | 30 s |
| 5 | **Full german hang** | 30 s |
| 6 | **German hang on rings** — rings allow rotation, which is kinder | 45 s |

Rung 3 is the one to be patient with. Being able to take weight off with your feet
means you can meet the end of your range *gradually* over weeks. Take that offer.

## Form cues

**Go slowly.** Ten seconds to get into the position, not one.

**Straight arms, but not locked hard.** Elbows extended, not hyper-extended or
forced.

**Keep pulling the shoulders down.** Even here, the shoulder is active. Passive
hanging in extension is where the load goes into the capsule.

**Breathe.** If you are holding your breath, you are too deep.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp front-of-shoulder pain | too deep, too fast | back off two rungs, go to feet-supported |
| Tingling in the hands | nerve tension or grip | stop, shake it out, reduce depth |
| Cannot get past halfway | normal early | weeks of rung 1 and 3; range comes back slowly |
| Elbow pain | straight-arm volume, not range | see the straight-arm lesson; reduce the week's total |

## Programming it

Three to four times a week, but treat it as **mobility, not strength**. Two or
three sets of 20–30 s. It should feel like a firm stretch, never like a maximum
effort.

Best placed at the end of a session or on a rest day, when you are warm.

## Target

**30 seconds in a full german hang, breathing normally, no sharp pain.** That is
the gate for the rest of module 3, and it is not negotiable.
