The first pull-up is the most requested skill in calisthenics and the most badly
trained. Almost everybody attacks it by attempting pull-ups they cannot do, which
trains nothing except disappointment.

This lesson is the method that actually works, and the physics that explains why.

## What a real pull-up is

- start from a **full dead hang**, arms straight
- **chin clearly over the bar** — not level with it
- **no kipping**, no leg drive, no swinging
- lower under control to a full hang

## The physics

![The four phases of a pull-up](/academy/figures/pullup-phases.svg)

The pull-up has four phases, and most people only know about two of them.

**Phase 1 — dead hang.** Arms straight, shoulders at your ears. Nothing is working
yet.

**Phase 2 — the scapular pull.** Pull your shoulders *down* while the arms stay
straight. Your body rises a few centimetres. Nothing bends.

![Dead hang compared with an active hang](/academy/figures/scapular-pull.svg)

This phase is the one people skip, and skipping it is why most failed pull-ups
stall a third of the way up. If your shoulder blades are not set before you bend
your arms, your lats are not in a position to contribute and the biceps take a
load they cannot finish.

**Phase 3 — the pull.** Elbows drive down and back.

**Phase 4 — the finish.** Chest toward the bar.

And the reason a first pull-up is trainable at all:

![Relative force capacity by contraction type](/academy/figures/force-capacity.svg)

**You can lower about 1.3× what you can lift.** If you cannot do a single pull-up,
you can almost certainly do a slow negative — and negatives load the exact
movement, through the exact range, at a force your tissue can produce. That is why
they build the first rep faster than anything else.

## The progression

| Rung | What to do | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dead hang** | 30 s |
| 2 | **Scapular pull** — straight arms, shoulders down | 3×8 |
| 3 | **Negative pull-up** — jump up, lower for 5 s | 3×5 |
| 4 | **Band-assisted pull-up** | 3×8 |
| 5 | **First pull-up** | 1 clean rep |
| 6 | **Pull-up** | 3×5 |
| 7 | **Pull-up** | 3×10 |
| 8 | **Paused pull-up** — 2 s at the top | 3×5 |
| 9 | **Weighted pull-up** | 3×5 at +10 kg |

## The first-rep protocol

If you are stuck between rungs 3 and 5, run this for six weeks:

**Three times a week:**
- scapular pulls — 3×8
- negatives — 5 singles, 5 s each, resting 90 s between
- band-assisted — 3×5 with the lightest band that lets you finish
- dead hang — 2×max

**Plus, on every other day:** one single attempt. Not a set. One honest attempt,
fresh, and then walk away. This is "greasing the groove" — frequent, non-fatiguing
practice of the exact skill, and it works remarkably well for a movement you are
on the edge of.

Most people who follow this get their first rep in 6–12 weeks. If you are at
eight weeks and nothing has moved, the usual culprit is that the negatives are too
fast: 5 seconds means five, counted.

## Form cues

**Set the shoulders first.** Every rep. Before the arms do anything.

**Elbows down and back**, not out to the sides.

**Chest to the bar**, not chin over the bar. It changes the whole path and it is
the habit that later makes a muscle-up possible.

**Legs together, glutes on, slightly hollow.** A swinging body is a body leaking
force.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stall a third of the way up | no scapular pull | rung 2, every session, before anything else |
| Stall just below the bar | the finish | paused reps at the top, plus negatives from the top |
| Swinging | no brace | hollow body; pause at the bottom of every rep |
| Elbow pain | volume ramp, or all chin-ups | vary grip, reduce volume, add the forearm work |
| Grip fails first | expected early | dead hangs, 3×max, at the end of sessions |
| Shoulders shrug at the top | no depression | scapular pulls, and pause at the top |

## Programming it

Three times a week while chasing the first rep; twice a week once you have it and
are adding volume.

Once you can do 10, stop adding reps and start adding load. Endless high-rep
pull-ups build endurance you do not need and elbow irritation you do not want.

## Target

**3 sets of 5 clean pull-ups from a dead hang, chest to bar.** Then archer and
weighted work.
