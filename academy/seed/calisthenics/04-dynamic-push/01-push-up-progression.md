The push-up is the base of every dynamic push skill. Done to a real standard it also
carries most people 80% of the way to their first dip.

## The standard

A rep counts when:

- Body is one straight line from head to heel — **no sagging hips, no piking up**.
- **Elbows track at ~45°** to the torso, not flared to 90°.
- Chest comes to **fist height** (about 8–10 cm off the floor) at the bottom.
- You lock out fully at the top and **protract** — push the upper back "away" so the
  shoulder blades spread.

## The progression

1. **Wall push-ups** → **incline push-ups** (hands on a bench/table). Lower the surface
   as you get stronger. *Target: 3×12 at a given height, then lower it.*
2. **Knee push-ups** — only if incline options aren't available; the straight-body incline
   is a better teacher.
3. **Full push-ups.** *Target: 3×12 strict.*
4. **Tempo push-ups** — 3s down, 1s pause at the bottom, up. Brutal, and the fastest way
   to build control. *Target: 3×8.*
5. **Feet-elevated push-ups** — shifts load toward the shoulders, bridges to pike and
   pseudo-planche work. *Target: 3×10.*
6. **Archer / one-arm progressions** — for later, once dips and PPPs are solid.

## Form cues

- **Hollow body.** Same brace as the hollow hold — ribs down, glutes on.
- **"Screw your hands into the floor"** — creates external rotation torque and protects
  the shoulders.
- **Full range or it doesn't count.** Half reps build a half skill.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hips sag first | Brace harder; drop to incline until you can hold the line |
| Head pokes forward, chest stays high | Lead with the chest, keep a neutral neck |
| Elbows flare to 90° | Tuck them toward 45°; imagine arrow-shaped, not T-shaped |
| Bouncing out of the bottom | Add a 1-second pause on every rep |
