The support hold is the first straight-arm position in the course, and the
gateway to the entire pushing half of the static track. Everything above it — the
L-sit, the planche lean, the handstand, the dip — assumes you own it.

It looks like nothing: you hold yourself up on two bars, arms locked. It is not
nothing. It is the position where your shoulders learn to be **active** under
load, and where most people discover that they have been hanging off their joints
rather than holding themselves with muscle.

## What it is

On parallettes, dip bars, or two stable surfaces of equal height:

- arms **locked straight**
- shoulders **pushed down** hard, away from the ears
- body **vertical**, hollow, ribs down, glutes tight
- legs together, toes pointed
- eyes forward, not down

## The physics

Your arms are struts. A locked elbow transmits force through bone with almost no
muscular cost — which is why you can hold a support far longer than you can hold
a bent-arm position at the same height.

The work is at the **shoulder**, and specifically it is the job of keeping the
shoulder girdle *pushed down*. Let that go and you sink between your shoulder
blades: the joint takes load passively, the muscles that should be working switch
off, and you are now resting on connective tissue. It is comfortable. It is also
exactly how people accumulate shoulder problems.

The cue "push the floor away" is not poetry. It is the difference between an
active support and a hang.

## The progression

| Rung | What to do | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Feet-assisted support** — bars low, feet on the floor taking some weight | 30 s |
| 2 | **Support hold, knees tucked** | 20 s |
| 3 | **Full support hold** | 30 s |
| 4 | **Support hold with shrugs** — sink and press up, scapula only, elbows locked | 10 reps |
| 5 | **Ring support hold** — rings turned out at the bottom | 20 s |
| 6 | **Weighted support** — light vest or belt | 20 s |

Rung 5 deserves a warning. Rings remove stability, so the shoulder has to control
rotation as well as height, and the jump in difficulty from bars is much larger
than it looks. Do not attempt it before a clean 30 s on bars.

## Form cues

**Push down, not up.** You are not shrugging your body upward; you are pressing
the bars down and away.

**Ribs down, glutes tight.** Without it you will hang in your lower back, and the
"support hold" becomes a passive slump with straight arms.

**Elbows locked and rotated.** Aim the inside of your elbow forward. It stacks
the joint and cues the whole arm to stay straight.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulders creep up to the ears | not pressing down | go back to rung 1; do shrug reps to feel the range |
| Elbows unlock under fatigue | triceps or a sagging brace | shorten the hold; stop the set at the first bend |
| Wrist pain | bars too wide, or an over-extended wrist | narrow the grip, use parallettes, do the wrist prep |
| Body swings forward | hips not braced | tuck the pelvis, squeeze the glutes |
| Rings wobble uncontrollably | normal | you are not ready for rings — go back to bars |

## Programming it

Three times a week. Accumulate **60–90 seconds of total hold time** per session,
in sets that end while the shape is still clean — so five sets of 15 s, not one
heroic set of 40 and three ugly ones.

Total hold time is the number to progress. Add about 10% a week.

## Target

**30 seconds, motionless, shoulders down, body vertical.** Then move to the L-sit
ladder.

## Self-check

- [ ] 30 s support hold with no shrug and no elbow bend
- [ ] 10 clean scapular shrugs in support
- [ ] No wrist pain during or after
- [ ] I can breathe normally in the hold — if you cannot, you are bracing too hard
