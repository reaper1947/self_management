The pull-up is the best single test of relative upper-body strength — and almost nobody
teaches it as a progression. This is the exact ladder to take you from "I can't do one" to
clean, controlled reps.

## The standard

- Start from a **full dead hang** — arms straight, shoulders active (not shrugged).
- **Chin clears the bar** with no kip, swing or knee jerk.
- **Lower under control** to a full hang. The lower half builds the strength.
- Grip shoulder-width, palms away (pronated).

## The progression

1. **Dead hang** — build grip and shoulder engagement. *Target: 45 s active hang.*
2. **Scapular pulls** — tiny pull-ups powered only by the shoulder blades, arms stay
   straight. *Target: 3×8 slow.*
3. **Negatives** — jump/step to the top, lower over a genuine 5-count. *Target: 5×1 at 5 s.*
4. **Band- or foot-assisted pull-ups** — use the least help that lets you do 5 clean reps;
   reduce the assistance every week.
5. **Full pull-ups** — *Target: 3×5 strict*, then build volume to 3×8–10.
6. **Weighted / archer pulls** — once 8–10 strict is easy (next lesson).

## Form cues

- **"Bend the bar"** — even with straight arms in the scapular pull, try to snap the bar.
  Fires the lats and sets the shoulders.
- **Lead with the chest**, not the chin. Chin-reaching wrecks your neck and shortens range.
- **Control the eccentric.** Falling the last few inches trains nothing.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Can't feel the back working | Weeks of scapular pulls until it's obvious |
| Negatives "fall" then slow at the end | The first inches out of the top are the point — control them |
| Kipping to get past the sticking point | Kipping hides the weakness; use assistance instead |
| Half reps (chin never clears / never full hang) | Full range or reset the rep |
