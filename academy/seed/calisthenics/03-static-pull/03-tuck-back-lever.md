The back lever is the first true lever in the course — the first time you hold
your body horizontal against a real lever arm.

## What it is

Hanging from a bar, rotated so your body is horizontal and **face down**, arms
straight behind you, shoulders in extension.

## The physics

![The back-lever progression, with measured lever arms](/academy/figures/back-lever-ladder.svg)

Mechanically this is the front lever mirrored: torque about the bar is `m · g · d`,
and the ladder works by shrinking d. The percentages come out the same, because
the arithmetic does not care which way up you are.

**Your shoulders very much care.** In a front lever the shoulder is in flexion —
strong, stable, well supported. In a back lever it is in **extension**, which is
the position you spent the last two lessons earning. That is the entire reason the
back lever gets trained after the german hang and skin the cat, and why it is
usually attempted before the front lever despite loading similar amounts: the
lever arm is manageable, but the joint position is not forgiving.

There is a second-order effect worth knowing: because your arms are behind you,
a small amount of the load is taken by the biceps as a *stretched* muscle. That is
comfortable at a tuck and much less so at full lay, and it is why the last two
rungs of this ladder hurt in a way the first two do not.

## The progression

| Rung | What to do | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **German hang** and **skin the cat** — prerequisites | 30 s / 5 reps |
| 2 | **Tuck back lever** — knees tight to chest, body horizontal | 15 s |
| 3 | **Advanced tuck back lever** — back flat, knees away from chest | 12 s |
| 4 | **One-leg back lever** | 8 s each side |
| 5 | **Straddle back lever** | 8 s |
| 6 | **Full back lever** | 5 s |

## Getting into it

From a german hang, **pull your hips up** until your body reaches horizontal.
Do not try to arrive by lowering from the top — that is the eccentric version, and
it is a later progression, not an entry.

Get there slowly, hold, and come down through the german hang with control.

## Form cues

**Body horizontal, not head-down.** Almost everyone holds theirs too steep for the
first few months, which is a shorter lever than they think. Film it once; the
angle is always worse than it feels.

**Shoulders down and back.** The position is extension, not a collapse.

**Hollow, not arched.** The whole body should be one flat plank.

**Straight arms, elbows rotated out.** Aim the inside of the elbow outward. It
protects the joint and keeps the arm honest.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Front-of-shoulder pain | too much lever too early | drop a rung; more german hang |
| Elbow pain | straight-arm volume | week off straight-arm work; see module 2, lesson 6 |
| Hips sag | brace | back to hollow holds; drop a rung |
| Cannot reach horizontal | strength, honestly | hold at whatever angle you can and progress the angle |
| Can hold tuck, nothing beyond | expected — the jump is real | use one-leg and half-lay as intermediates |

## Programming it

Twice a week. 4–6 sets, each ending while the shape is good. A set is over when
your hips drop or your arms bend, not when you are out of effort.

Counts toward the straight-arm weekly budget, and this one adds up fast.

## Target

**15 seconds in a tuck back lever with the body genuinely horizontal.** Then the
tuck front lever.
