A plan you follow beats a better plan you do not. This lesson gives you three
templates and the rules for adapting them.

## The rules that apply to every template

**One static focus, one dynamic focus.** At any time. More than that and progress
on all of them slows.

**Statics twice a week, dynamics two or three times.** Tissue timescales, from
the last lesson.

**Skill before strength before conditioning.** Handstands and muscle-up
transitions come first, while you are fresh. Conditioning goes last, if at all.

**At least one full rest day.** Two if you are over 35, sleeping badly, or new to
this.

**Joint prep every single session.** Ten minutes. Never cut.

## Template A — three days a week

The best starting point for almost everyone, and a perfectly good permanent
answer.

**Monday — Push focus**
1. joint prep — 10 min
2. handstand practice — 10 min
3. static: support hold or L-sit — 4 sets
4. dynamic: dips — 4 sets
5. push-ups — 3 sets
6. hollow holds — 3 sets

**Wednesday — Pull focus**
1. joint prep — 10 min
2. handstand practice — 10 min
3. static: german hang or tuck lever — 4 sets
4. dynamic: pull-ups — 4 sets
5. rows or scapular pulls — 3 sets
6. hanging leg raises — 3 sets

**Friday — Mixed**
1. joint prep — 10 min
2. handstand practice — 10 min
3. explosive: fast pull-ups or clapping push-ups — 4 sets
4. static: your current focus — 3 sets
5. dips and pull-ups — 3 sets each
6. hollow and superman — 2 sets each

## Template B — four days a week

For people already training consistently for six months or more.

- **Monday** — Push strength (dips, pike push-ups, planche lean)
- **Tuesday** — Pull strength (pull-ups, rows, front lever)
- **Thursday** — Skill (handstand, muscle-up transitions, explosive)
- **Friday** — Push and pull volume, lighter, higher reps

## Template C — six days a week, short sessions

For handstand-focused training, or anyone who finds daily practice easier to keep
than long sessions.

- **Every day:** 15 min — joint prep and handstand practice
- **Mon / Wed / Fri:** plus 30 min strength, alternating push and pull
- **Tue / Thu / Sat:** plus 15 min statics or mobility
- **Sunday:** rest completely

This one works beautifully for balance skills and badly for people who cannot
stop themselves adding "just one more set."

## A session, in order

1. **Joint prep** — 10 min, never skipped
2. **Skill work** — handstands, transitions. Fresh, low fatigue.
3. **Explosive** — if it is in today's plan
4. **Statics** — holds, levers. Before you are tired; shape degrades fast.
5. **Dynamics** — the main strength work
6. **Accessory** — rows, core, forearms
7. **Decompress** — hangs, gentle stretching — 5 min

## How to fit the course into it

Your two focuses come from the assessment. Everything else is maintenance:

| Focus | Where it goes |
|---|---|
| static | step 4, 4–6 sets, twice a week |
| dynamic | step 5, 4–5 sets, two or three times a week |
| everything else | 2–3 maintenance sets, once a week |

Maintenance is real. You will not lose a skill doing two sets a week of it.

## When life interferes

**Missed a session** — do not merge it into the next one. Skip it and carry on.

**Only 20 minutes** — joint prep, then your two focuses, two sets each. Done.

**Travelling with no bar** — push-ups, handstand work, hollow holds, L-sit on the
floor. The static track survives travel much better than the dynamic one.

**Sick** — nothing above the neck rule; if it is in your chest, do not train.
