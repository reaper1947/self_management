Before you train anything you need to know where you actually are — not where you
were at your fittest, and not where you would like to be. Everything that follows
is calibrated off these numbers.

Do the whole assessment in one session, rested. Write every number down. It takes
about forty minutes.

## Before you start

Warm up properly: five minutes of easy movement, then the joint prep from the
next lesson. An assessment done cold measures your warm-up, not your strength.

Stop any test the moment form breaks. A rep with a bent arm in a straight-arm
test is not a rep, and counting it means training the wrong rung for weeks.

## The seven tests

### 1. Dead hang — grip and shoulder tolerance

Hang from the bar, arms straight, feet clear of the floor. Time it to the first
moment your grip genuinely slips.

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| under 15 s | grip is your limiter before anything else is |
| 15–30 s | normal untrained starting point |
| 30–60 s | ready for everything in module 3 |
| over 60 s | grip will not limit you for a long time |

### 2. Push-up — horizontal push

Maximum consecutive reps. Chest to fist-height off the floor, body in one line,
no sagging hips, full lockout at the top. Stop at the first rep that breaks form.

| Result | Start at |
|---|---|
| 0 | incline push-ups |
| 1–5 | knee push-ups for volume, full push-ups for quality |
| 6–15 | full push-ups, begin the progression |
| 16+ | move toward dips and decline variants |

### 3. Pull-up — vertical pull

Maximum consecutive reps from a full dead hang, chin clearly over the bar, no
kipping and no leg drive.

| Result | Start at |
|---|---|
| 0 | negatives and band-assisted work |
| 1–4 | greasing the groove, and the first-rep protocol |
| 5–10 | volume work, then archer and weighted |
| 11+ | weighted pull-ups and muscle-up preparation |

### 4. Hollow hold — trunk

Lie on your back, lower back pressed flat into the floor, shoulders and legs
lifted. Time it to the moment your lower back lifts off — **not** to the moment
you get tired. The back leaving the floor is the end of the test.

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| under 15 s | this is your priority, before any lever work |
| 15–45 s | normal; train it alongside everything else |
| over 45 s | your trunk will not be the limiter |

### 5. Support hold — straight-arm push

On parallettes, dip bars, or two stable surfaces: arms locked, shoulders pushed
down away from your ears, body vertical. Time to the first shrug or bent elbow.

| Result | Start at |
|---|---|
| under 10 s | build the support hold itself first |
| 10–30 s | ready for the L-sit ladder |
| over 30 s | ready for planche lean |

### 6. Shoulder extension — the safety test

Stand with your hands behind you on a low bar or bench, and slowly let your
weight sink until you feel a firm stretch across the front of the shoulder. Do
not push into pain. Note roughly how far your shoulders travel behind your torso.

This is not a strength test. It gates module 3 entirely: **if your shoulders
cannot get into extension comfortably, do not train skin the cat or back
levers.** That module explains exactly why.

### 7. Wrist tolerance

Kneel, hands flat on the floor, fingers forward, and lean your weight over them.
Repeat with fingers pointing back toward your knees. Note any pain or pinching in
either direction.

Wrists gate the entire handstand and planche line. Nothing in this course injures
more people than untrained wrists meeting a lot of load.

## Turning seven numbers into a starting point

Take your **lowest** relevant score, not your best. A chain trains at the speed of
its weakest link, and the weakest link is where injuries happen.

Then pick exactly two things:

- **one static focus** — support hold, L-sit, german hang, or a tuck lever
- **one dynamic focus** — push-up, dip, pull-up, or muscle-up preparation

Write them at the top of your log. They stay there for at least four weeks.

## Re-testing

Re-test all seven **every six weeks**, in the same order, rested, warmed up the
same way. Not more often — shorter intervals measure how you slept, not how you
adapted.
