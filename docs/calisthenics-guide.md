# Calisthenics: Zero to Expert

_From your first push-up to the planche — static and dynamic bodyweight strength, built as progressions._

This guide is the complete written companion to the course. It is organised into two
parallel tracks — **static movements** (holding your bodyweight still) and **dynamic
movements** (moving it through range) — wrapped in foundations at the start and programming
at the end.

---

<!-- FILE: 01-foundations/01-how-this-works.md -->
Welcome. This course turns "I train sometimes" into a deliberate practice that takes you
from the basics to expert-level bodyweight skills.

## The two tracks

Every calisthenics skill is one of two things:

- **Static** — holding your bodyweight still against gravity: the support hold, L-sit,
  handstand, german hang, back lever, front lever, planche.
- **Dynamic** — moving your bodyweight through a range: the push-up, dip, pull-up,
  muscle-up, explosive work.

They train different qualities (static = tendon stiffness and straight-arm strength;
dynamic = contractile strength and control through range), so the course runs them in
**parallel**. You'll always have one static and one dynamic focus at a time.

## How a lesson is built

1. **What it is** and why it matters.
2. **The progression** — an ordered ladder of easier-to-harder variations. You only ever
   train the step you're on, plus a taste of the next.
3. **Form cues** — the two or three things that actually matter.
4. **Common mistakes** — how the movement fails, and the fix.
5. **A target** — the concrete standard that says "move to the next step."
6. **Self-check** — a short checklist before you progress.

## The rules of progression

- **One step at a time.** Chasing the end skill is how people plateau for years.
- **Quality first.** A clean rep with a 2-second pause beats five sloppy ones.
- **Progress one variable per week** — reps, seconds, leverage, or load. Not all at once.
- **Log everything.** "Felt hard" is not data. "3×5 negatives, 4s each" is.

## What you need

A **pull-up bar**, a **floor**, and ideally **parallettes** (or two low, stable surfaces)
and a **resistance band**. That's the whole kit. Rings are a bonus, not a requirement.

Next: an honest assessment so your training actually fits you.

<!-- FILE: 01-foundations/02-the-assessment.md -->
Do this once, fresh, warmed up but not tired. Write every number down — this is your
baseline and you'll retest it every 6 weeks.

## The tests

Rest 2–3 minutes between tests. Strict form only — if a rep is ugly, it doesn't count.

| # | Test | How to judge it |
|---|------|-----------------|
| 1 | Max **push-ups** | Chest to fist height, straight line head-to-heel, no sagging hips |
| 2 | Max **pull-ups** | Dead hang start, chin over bar, no kip or leg swing |
| 3 | Max **dip** (parallel bar or bench) | Shoulders below elbows at the bottom |
| 4 | **Dead hang** for time | Active shoulders, feet off the floor |
| 5 | **Hollow body hold** for time | Lower back flat to the floor the entire time |
| 6 | **Wall handstand** (chest to wall) for time | Straight body, no banana |
| 7 | Max **bodyweight squats** | Hip crease below knee |

## Reading your result

| | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push-ups | < 15 | 15–35 | 35+ |
| Pull-ups | 0–4 | 5–12 | 12+ |
| Dips | 0–6 | 6–15 | 15+ |
| Dead hang | < 30 s | 30–60 s | 60 s+ |
| Hollow hold | < 20 s | 20–45 s | 45 s+ |
| Wall handstand | < 20 s | 20–45 s | 45 s+ |

## What to do with it

- **Any test where you scored 0** (a common one: pull-ups, dips) — that movement's
  progression starts at step 1 for you. That's normal and expected.
- **Your weakest relative category** (push vs pull vs core) gets the extra set.
- **Retest the same 7 tests every 6 weeks.** If 4+ improved, your program is working.
  If nothing moved, you're under-recovering or not progressing the load.

## Self-check

- [ ] I have a written number for all 7 tests
- [ ] I know which movements start at step 1 for me
- [ ] I've put a retest date 6 weeks out in my calendar

<!-- FILE: 01-foundations/03-joint-prep-and-mobility.md -->
Calisthenics loads your wrists, elbows and shoulders in ranges that gym training never
touches. Ten minutes of preparation before every session keeps you training for years.

## Wrists (before any push / handstand work)

- **Wrist rocks** — on all fours, hands flat, rock forward and back. 2×20.
- **Palm-down and palm-up rocks** — fingers pointing at knees, then away, then out. 10 each.
- **Fist and finger-tip holds** — 20 s each to build the tissue for weight on the hands.

## Elbows (before any straight-arm work — levers, planche)

- **Straight-arm band pull-aparts** — 2×15, elbows locked.
- **Weighted or bodyweight elbow flexion/extension** through full range, slow. 2×10.
- Ramp straight-arm loads by **no more than ~10% a week**. Elbows warn late and heal slow.

## Shoulders (before pulling / german hang / dips)

- **Scapular push-ups** and **scapular pulls** — 2×10 each. Own protraction and depression.
- **Band dislocates** — wide grip, slow, 2×10. Builds the overhead and behind-the-back range.
- **Prone Y-T-W raises** — 2×8 each for the mid- and lower-trap.

## The daily minimum

Wrist rocks · scapular pulls · scapular push-ups · band dislocates · 30 s active hang.
Under 5 minutes. Do it even on rest days.

## Self-check

- [ ] I have a fixed 5–10 min prep routine I do every session
- [ ] Wrists tolerate bodyweight on the hands without pain
- [ ] I can do 10 slow band dislocates through full range

<!-- FILE: 01-foundations/04-hollow-body-and-bracing.md -->
The hollow body is the shape your torso holds during almost every calisthenics skill — the
handstand, the front lever, a strict pull-up. It means **ribs down, lower back flat,
everything braced**.

## The position

Lie on your back. Press your lower back into the floor so there is **no gap**. Lift your
shoulder blades and legs a few inches off the ground, arms overhead. If your back arches,
you've gone too far — raise the arms and legs higher until the low back stays flat.

## The progression

1. **Tuck hollow** — knees tucked, arms by your sides. Build to 3×40 s.
2. **One leg extended** — alternate. 3×30 s.
3. **Legs straight, arms by sides.** 3×30 s.
4. **Full hollow** — legs straight and together, arms overhead. *Target: 3×30 s.*
5. **Hollow rocks** — same shape, rock head-to-toe from the mid-back. 3×15.

## Why it matters

A leaking hollow is why beginners "banana" in a handstand and swing in a pull-up. Fix it on
the floor first — it is free progress that transfers to everything.

## The brace, everywhere

The same brace applies standing: **ribs stacked over hips, glutes lightly on, 360° of
abdominal tension** (as if about to be poked in the stomach). This is the default trunk
position for supports, levers and presses.

## Self-check

- [ ] Full hollow hold 30 s with the low back glued to the floor
- [ ] 15 hollow rocks without the shape breaking
- [ ] I can find the same brace standing, on command

<!-- FILE: 02-static-support/01-support-hold.md -->
The support hold — arms locked, body above your hands on parallettes, rings or dip bars —
is the foundation of every static push skill. If you can't own it, the L-sit, planche and
handstand all have a weak base.

## What it trains

- **Scapular depression** — pushing your shoulders down away from your ears under load.
- **Straight-arm elbow and biceps-tendon tolerance** — the tissue quality static skills need.
- **Anterior core** — keeping the ribs down so you don't arch.

## The progression

1. **Bench/floor support** — hands on a bench, feet on the floor, take some weight off.
   Build to 3×30 s.
2. **Parallette support hold** — full bodyweight, feet off the floor. *Target: 3×30 s.*
3. **Ring support hold** — the instability adds a large demand. Turn the rings slightly out
   ("RTO") as you get stronger. *Target: 3×20 s RTO.*
4. **Weighted support hold** — small dip belt, 5–10 kg, parallettes. Builds advanced planche strength.

## Form cues

- **"Push the floor away"** — actively press down even though your arms are already straight.
- **Shoulders down, chest tall.** Nowhere near your ears.
- **Ribs down, glutes on.** Slight posterior pelvic tilt — no arching.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Shrugged shoulders | Think "long neck," actively depress the scapulae |
| Arched lower back | Squeeze glutes, tuck the pelvis, brace the hollow |
| Bent elbows creeping in | Drop to an easier step |
| Rings flailing | Press them into your sides; build time before RTO |

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×30 s on parallettes, shoulders clearly depressed, no arch
- [ ] I can breathe normally in the hold
- [ ] Elbows fully straight the entire time

<!-- FILE: 02-static-support/02-l-sit-progression.md -->
The L-sit — supported on your hands, legs straight out in front — builds brutal core
compression and straight-arm strength, and it's the gateway to the V-sit and manna.

## The progression

1. **Foot-supported L-sit** — on parallettes, heels on the floor, press down hard. 3×20 s.
2. **Tuck L-sit** — knees tucked to chest, hips fully off the floor. *Target: 3×20 s.*
3. **One-leg-extended L-sit** — alternate legs. 3×15 s each.
4. **Full L-sit** — both legs straight, parallel to the floor. *Target: 3×15 s.*
5. **Raised / advanced** — L-sit on the floor (harder — less clearance), then work toward the V-sit.

## Form cues

- **Depress and protract** the shoulders — the same "push the floor away" as the support hold.
- **Actively pull the toes up** and squeeze the quads; compression comes from the hip flexors *and* the abs.
- **Posterior pelvic tilt** — round the low back slightly to bring the legs up, don't just lift with the hip.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Legs drift below parallel | Regress to tuck; build compression with seated leg lifts |
| Shoulders shrug | Cue depression first, then lift the legs |
| Holding breath | Breathe shallow but continuously |

## Accessory work

Seated compression lifts (sit on floor, legs straight, lift heels off ground) and
banded leg raises. 3×10, twice a week.

## Self-check

- [ ] Full L-sit 15 s on parallettes, legs at or above parallel
- [ ] Shoulders stay depressed the whole hold
- [ ] I can do 3×10 seated compression lifts

<!-- FILE: 02-static-support/03-tuck-planche-and-lean.md -->
The planche — body held horizontal, supported only by the hands, no feet — is the peak
static push skill. The road there is the **planche lean** and the **tuck planche**.

## The progression

1. **Planche lean** — in a push-up plank on parallettes, shift your shoulders forward past
   your hands, keeping arms straight and scapulae protracted. The further forward, the
   harder. *Target: 3×20 s at a lean where your shoulders are well ahead of your wrists.*
2. **Frog stand / crow** — knees on elbows, balance. Builds the wrist and straight-arm base. 3×30 s.
3. **Tuck planche** — knees tucked tight to chest, hips at shoulder height, **arms straight**,
   back rounded (protracted). *Target: 3×15 s.*
4. **Advanced tuck planche** — hips open, back flat, knees still bent. 3×10 s.
5. **Straddle planche** → **full planche** — years of work; covered in the advanced lessons.

## Form cues

- **Arms dead straight.** A bent-arm "planche" is a different (easier) skill and stalls progress.
- **Protract hard** — push the upper back to the ceiling, round it.
- **Posterior pelvic tilt**, glutes on, point the toes.
- **Lean is everything.** Planche strength = how far forward your shoulders can go past your hands.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Elbows bend under load | Reduce lean / regress to lean holds only |
| Hips sag below shoulders | Push harder into protraction; think "pike up" |
| Wrist pain | More wrist prep; use parallettes; build frog stand time |

## Programming

Planche work is high-intensity, low-volume. 2 sessions/week, 4–6 sets of 5–15 s holds,
full rest between. Progress by leaning 1–2 cm further or adding 2–3 s.

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×20 s planche lean, shoulders clearly past the wrists, arms straight
- [ ] 3×15 s tuck planche, arms straight, back rounded
- [ ] No elbow or wrist pain 24 h after a session

<!-- FILE: 02-static-support/04-handstand-wall.md -->
The handstand is a static skill *and* a balance skill. You build the strength and shape
against a wall first, then transfer it to freestanding.

## Two wall drills — do both

1. **Chest-to-wall handstand** — walk your feet up the wall until your chest and stomach
   face it and your body is vertical and straight. This trains the correct **line**.
   *Target: 3×45 s, straight body.*
2. **Back-to-wall handstand** — kick up with your back near the wall, only heels touching.
   This lets you feel balance and practice small corrections. *Target: 3×45 s.*

## The line (this is the whole skill)

From hands to toes: **wrists stacked under shoulders under hips under heels**. Ribs down,
posterior pelvic tilt, shoulders fully "open" (pushed up into the ears — the one place you
*want* to shrug), fingers actively gripping the floor.

## Balance basics

- Corrections come from the **fingers and wrists**, not the hips. Falling forward → press
  the fingertips. Falling back → let the heel of the hand take more load.
- Look at the floor between your hands, not forward.

## Progression to freestanding

Once you own 45 s chest-to-wall with a straight line: practice **kick-ups to balance** in
an open space, aiming to find 1–2 seconds of freestanding time. Bail by turning out
(cartwheel) — never collapse straight down.

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×45 s chest-to-wall, straight line (film it from the side)
- [ ] 3×45 s back-to-wall with only heels touching
- [ ] I have a safe bail (turn-out) and use it

<!-- FILE: 02-static-support/05-handstand-freestanding.md -->
Freestanding handstand balance is a practice, not a strength exercise — done in short,
frequent, fresh doses.

## Daily practice structure (10–15 min)

1. Wrist prep — 2 min.
2. **Wall line hold** — 2×30 s chest-to-wall to reset the shape.
3. **Kick-up practice** — 15–25 attempts to balance, resting as needed. Quality of entry
   matters: a controlled kick-up that stacks over the shoulders is the goal, not a hard kick.
4. **Bail practice** if new — deliberately over-balance and turn out 5×.

## The balance skill

- **Hollow, not arched.** Most people over-arch and "banana." Film from the side weekly.
- **Micro-corrections with the hands.** Weight forward on fingers to stop a forward fall;
  press through the heel of the hand for a backward fall.
- **Stack, then hold.** Don't chase a bad entry — bail and re-kick.

## Milestones

| Level | Standard |
|---|---|
| Beginner | 1–3 s freestanding |
| Intermediate | 10–20 s consistent, can self-correct |
| Advanced | 30 s+, straight line, presses to handstand |

## Next skills from here

- **Press to handstand** (straddle or pike) — strength + compression + balance.
- **Handstand push-up** — bent-arm strength on top of the line.

## Self-check

- [ ] Consistent 10 s freestanding holds
- [ ] I practice 4–6 days/week in short sessions
- [ ] Side-on video shows a straight (not arched) line

<!-- FILE: 03-static-pull/01-german-hang.md -->
The german hang (a.k.a. skin-the-cat hold) is a hang behind your body with straight arms —
your shoulders in deep extension. It's the single best preparation for back lever, skin the
cat and any advanced ring work, and it builds bombproof shoulders if you approach it slowly.

## Safety first

Deep shoulder extension under load is a position people rush and hurt themselves in.
**Never bounce, never load it cold, and progress by inches.** Tingling in the hands or a
sharp pinch at the front of the shoulder means back off immediately.

## The progression

1. **Passive shoulder-extension stretch, feet supported** — hold a bar behind you at hip
   height, walk the feet forward to take a light stretch. 3×30 s, daily.
2. **Skin-the-cat to tuck, lower to german hang with feet on a box** — control 80% of your
   weight, feet bail you out. Build to 3×20 s.
3. **Full german hang, feet free, knees tucked.** *Target: 3×30 s calm breathing.*
4. **Straight-body german hang.** Legs together and straight. This is the standard.
5. **German hang pull-out** — from the hang, pull back to a regular hang with straight arms.

## Form cues

- **Straight arms, always.** Bending the elbows hides the weakness you're building.
- **Actively pull the rings/bar apart and down** — don't just dangle in the end range.
- **Breathe.** Holding your breath means the intensity is too high.

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×30 s straight-body german hang, no pinch, breathing normally
- [ ] I can get in and out under control with straight arms
- [ ] Zero tingling or numbness in the hands

<!-- FILE: 03-static-pull/02-skin-the-cat.md -->
Skin the cat is a full rotation through shoulder flexion into extension and back — the
dynamic sibling of the german hang, and a fantastic shoulder health and control builder.

## The progression

1. **Tuck skin-the-cat, feet-assisted** — from a hang, pull the knees to the chest, rotate
   the hips back and over until the toes point down behind you, then reverse. Use a box or
   a partner to offload. 3×5.
2. **Tuck skin-the-cat, unassisted.** *Target: 3×5 slow and controlled.*
3. **Straddle skin-the-cat** — legs wide, more leverage. 3×5.
4. **Straight-body skin-the-cat.** 3×3–5.
5. **Rings, slow eccentric into german hang** and back — the strength standard.

## Form cues

- **Straight arms throughout.** The elbows want to bend at the hardest point (horizontal) — don't let them.
- **Move slowly, especially through the bottom** (arms level with the body) where leverage is worst.
- Keep the shoulders **actively engaged** — pull down and in, never fully passive.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Whipping through with momentum | Slow every rep to a 3-count each way |
| Elbows bend at horizontal | Regress to tuck / add feet assistance |
| Shoulder pinch behind | Spend more weeks on german hang first |

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×5 unassisted tuck skin-the-cat, straight arms, controlled
- [ ] I can pause for 2 s at the bottom (arms horizontal) without the elbows bending
- [ ] Shoulders feel worked, not pinched, afterward

<!-- FILE: 03-static-pull/03-tuck-back-lever.md -->
The back lever — body horizontal, face down, hanging from a bar behind you with straight
arms — is the first "impressive" straight-arm pull skill. Build it from the german hang.

## The progression

1. **German hang hold** — the prerequisite. 3×30 s straight-body (previous lesson).
2. **Tuck back lever** — from an inverted hang, lower until your back is horizontal, knees
   tucked tight, **arms straight**. *Target: 3×15 s.*
3. **Advanced tuck back lever** — open the hips so the thighs are in line with the torso,
   knees still bent. 3×10 s.
4. **Straddle back lever** — legs wide and straight. 3×8 s.
5. **Full back lever** — legs together and straight. The standard.

## Form cues

- **Arms locked.** Check with a partner or video — elbows love to sneak into a bend.
- **Protract and depress** the shoulders; think about pulling the bar toward your hips.
- **Posterior pelvic tilt**, glutes and legs tight — no arching.
- Grip: start **supinated** (palms up) which is stronger for beginners, progress to pronated.

## Programming

2×/week, 4–5 sets of 5–15 s holds, long rest. Also train the **eccentric**: from inverted
hang, lower to horizontal over 5 s, 3×3. Progress by opening the tuck or adding seconds.

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×15 s solid tuck back lever, arms verified straight
- [ ] 3×3 slow (5 s) lowers to horizontal
- [ ] Can hold advanced tuck (hips open) for 8 s

<!-- FILE: 03-static-pull/04-tuck-front-lever.md -->
The front lever — body horizontal, face up, hanging from a bar with straight arms — is the
benchmark straight-arm pull skill and the one most people want.

## The progression

1. **Tuck front lever** — from a hang, pull the knees to the chest and bring the body to
   horizontal, **arms straight**, back rounded. *Target: 3×15 s.*
2. **Advanced tuck** — open the hips, flatten the back, knees bent, shins parallel to floor. 3×10 s.
3. **One-leg front lever** — one leg extended, one tucked. Alternate. 3×8 s each.
4. **Straddle front lever** — both legs straight and wide. 3×8 s.
5. **Full front lever** — legs together and straight.

## Form cues

- **Straight arms — non-negotiable.** Bent-arm front lever is a different skill.
- **Depress and slightly retract** the scapulae, and **pull the bar toward your thighs** —
  this "straight-arm pulldown" feeling is the engine of the skill.
- Hollow body, glutes on, posterior pelvic tilt.

## Key accessory: straight-arm pulldowns

Standing, band or cable overhead, arms straight, pull the band down to your thighs by
depressing the shoulders. 3×12, twice a week — this directly builds the front lever.

## Programming

2×/week. Holds (4–6×5–15 s) plus **front lever raises** (from a hang, keep arms straight,
raise to tuck-lever and lower, 3×5) and **eccentrics** (lower from inverted to horizontal
over 5 s).

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×15 s tuck front lever, arms straight, back rounded
- [ ] 3×12 straight-arm band pulldowns with control
- [ ] Advanced tuck (hips open) for 10 s

<!-- FILE: 03-static-pull/05-advanced-levers.md -->
Taking the front and back lever from tuck to full is a 1–3 year project. Here is how the
progression is structured and programmed.

## The leverage ladder

Each step lengthens the lever (moves mass away from the axis), roughly:

`tuck → advanced tuck → single-leg → straddle (wide → narrow) → full`

Move up a step when you can hold the current one **for ~15 s across 3 sets** with verified
straight arms and a clean line.

## The three exercises per skill

1. **Isometric holds** — 4–6 sets of 5–15 s at your current step, plus 1–2 sets at the
   next-harder step for 3–5 s ("exposure").
2. **Eccentrics** — lower from a stronger position (inverted hang or tuck) to horizontal
   over 4–6 s. 3×3.
3. **Raises / pulls** — front lever raises, back lever pull-outs, straight-arm pulldowns.
   3×5–8. These build the concentric strength that makes holds feel easy.

## Weekly structure

Two pull-focus days. Levers are **straight-arm** work: separate them by 48 h from heavy
**bent-arm** pulling (weighted pull-ups) so the elbows recover. Deload every 4–6 weeks.

## Straddle → full is mostly hips

Closing a straddle adds surprising difficulty. Bridge it with **narrow straddle** holds and
**one-leg** holds (much harder than they look). Point the toes — it genuinely matters for
the line and the leverage.

## Self-check

- [ ] Straddle front **or** back lever held 10 s+
- [ ] I run holds + eccentrics + raises every week, not just holds
- [ ] Elbows and biceps tendons are pain-free with the current volume

<!-- FILE: 04-dynamic-push/01-push-up-progression.md -->
The push-up is the base of every dynamic push skill. Done to a real standard it also
carries most people 80% of the way to their first dip.

## The standard

A rep counts when:

- Body is one straight line from head to heel — **no sagging hips, no piking up**.
- **Elbows track at ~45°** to the torso, not flared to 90°.
- Chest comes to **fist height** (about 8–10 cm off the floor).
- You lock out fully at the top and **protract** — spread the shoulder blades.

## The progression

1. **Wall → incline push-ups** (hands on a bench/table); lower the surface as you get stronger. 3×12, then lower.
2. **Full push-ups.** *Target: 3×12 strict.*
3. **Tempo push-ups** — 3 s down, 1 s pause, up. *Target: 3×8.*
4. **Feet-elevated push-ups** — shifts load to the shoulders. *Target: 3×10.*
5. **Archer / one-arm progressions** — later, once dips and PPPs are solid.

## Form cues

- **Hollow body** — ribs down, glutes on.
- **"Screw your hands into the floor"** — creates external-rotation torque, protects the shoulders.
- **Full range or it doesn't count.**

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hips sag first | Brace harder; regress to incline |
| Head pokes forward, chest high | Lead with the chest, neutral neck |
| Elbows flare to 90° | Tuck toward 45°; arrow-shaped not T-shaped |
| Bouncing out of the bottom | 1-second pause every rep |

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×12 strict full push-ups, straight line, chest to fist height
- [ ] 3×8 tempo push-ups (3 s down + 1 s pause)
- [ ] Full push-up with a 2-second bottom pause, no collapse

<!-- FILE: 04-dynamic-push/02-parallel-bar-dips.md -->
The dip is the deadlift of upper-body pushing — big loads on the chest, triceps and
shoulders. It's also the most-injured calisthenics movement, so the ramp matters.

## The progression

1. **Support hold** (from the static track) — own 3×30 s before you dip.
2. **Negative dips** — jump to the top, lower over 3–5 s to shoulders-below-elbows. 4×3.
3. **Band- or foot-assisted dips** — least assistance for 5 clean reps; reduce weekly.
4. **Full dips.** *Target: 3×8 to full depth.*
5. **Weighted dips** — add load once 3×10 bodyweight is easy. Progress ~2.5 kg/2 weeks.
6. **Ring dips** (turned out) — a big step up in stability demand.

## Form cues

- **Depth:** shoulders below the elbows — but no deeper than pain-free, especially early.
- **Slight forward lean** for more chest, upright for more triceps. Pick one and be consistent.
- **Shoulders down and back** at the bottom — never let them shrug up to the ears.
- Elbows track back, not flared wide.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Shoulders shrug at the bottom | Regress; drill the support hold + scapular depression |
| Bouncing out of the bottom | Pause 1 s at depth; slow the descent |
| Chasing depth before strength | Half-depth strict beats full-depth sloppy |
| Front-of-shoulder pain | Reduce depth and lean; more warm-up; deload |

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×8 full-depth strict dips, shoulders stable
- [ ] 4×3 slow (4 s) negatives to full depth
- [ ] No shoulder pain the day after

<!-- FILE: 04-dynamic-push/03-straight-bar-dips.md -->
Straight-bar dips (on a single bar, like the bottom half of a muscle-up) build the exact
strength and shoulder position the **muscle-up transition** needs.

## Setup

A chest-height bar. Start supported on straight arms, bar at your hips, body leaning
forward *over* the bar, legs slightly in front for counterbalance.

## The progression

1. **Straight-bar support hold**, leaning forward. 3×20 s.
2. **Negative straight-bar dips** — from support, lower under control until your chest
   touches the bar, 3–5 s. 4×3.
3. **Assisted straight-bar dips** (band or feet). 3×5.
4. **Full straight-bar dips.** *Target: 3×8.*
5. **Explosive straight-bar dips** — press fast out of the bottom; direct muscle-up prep. 4×3.

## Form cues

- **Lean forward** — chest travels forward and down to the bar, not straight down. This is
  different from parallel-bar dips and it's the point.
- Wrists will feel it — build straight-bar support time first, wrap the thumbs over.
- Keep a **hollow body**; don't let the legs swing.

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×8 full straight-bar dips, chest to bar, forward lean
- [ ] 4×3 explosive reps with a clear "pop" out of the bottom
- [ ] Comfortable in a 20 s forward-leaning straight-bar support

<!-- FILE: 04-dynamic-push/04-pike-and-ppp.md -->
Pike push-ups and pseudo-planche push-ups (PPPs) are the dynamic bridge between the
push-up and the two hardest push skills: the handstand push-up and the planche.

## Pike push-ups → handstand push-up

1. **Floor pike push-up** — hips high, head travels to the floor between the hands. 3×8.
2. **Feet-elevated pike push-up** — more vertical, more load. 3×8.
3. **Wall handstand push-up, partial** — chest-to-wall, lower a few inches, press. 3×5.
4. **Full wall handstand push-up.** *Target: 3×5.*
5. **Freestanding HSPU** — advanced; needs a solid freestanding handstand first.

## Pseudo-planche push-ups → planche

1. **PPP** — push-up with hands at your waist, fingers pointing back or out, shoulders
   leaning well forward past the hands, **arms bending only after a big lean**. 3×8.
2. Increase the **lean** each week (shoulders further past the wrists).
3. **Elevated PPP** (feet raised) and **tuck-planche push-ups** — deep into planche territory.

## Form cues

- **Protract hard** in both — round the upper back, push it to the ceiling.
- PPP: the difficulty is the *lean*, not the depth. Small range, big forward lean.
- Wrist prep is mandatory before either.

## Self-check

- [ ] 3×8 feet-elevated pike push-ups, controlled
- [ ] 3×5 partial wall HSPU
- [ ] 3×8 PPPs with a visible forward shoulder lean, arms straightening at the top

<!-- FILE: 05-dynamic-pull/01-pull-up-progression.md -->
The pull-up is the best single test of relative upper-body strength — and almost nobody
teaches it as a progression. This is the exact ladder from "I can't do one" to clean reps.

## The standard

- Start from a **full dead hang** — arms straight, shoulders active.
- **Chin clears the bar** with no kip, swing or knee jerk.
- **Lower under control** to a full hang.
- Grip shoulder-width, palms away (pronated).

## The progression

1. **Dead hang** — *Target: 45 s active hang.*
2. **Scapular pulls** — tiny pull-ups from the shoulder blades, arms straight. *3×8 slow.*
3. **Negatives** — jump/step to the top, lower over a genuine 5-count. *5×1 at 5 s.*
4. **Band- or foot-assisted pull-ups** — least help for 5 clean reps; reduce weekly.
5. **Full pull-ups** — *3×5 strict*, then build to 3×8–10.
6. **Weighted / archer pulls** — next lesson.

## Form cues

- **"Bend the bar"** — even with straight arms, try to snap it. Fires the lats.
- **Lead with the chest**, not the chin.
- **Control the eccentric** — falling the last few inches trains nothing.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Can't feel the back | Weeks of scapular pulls |
| Negatives "fall" then slow at the end | Control the first inches out of the top |
| Kipping past the sticking point | Use assistance instead — kipping hides the weakness |
| Half reps | Full range or reset the rep |

## Self-check

- [ ] 45 s active dead hang
- [ ] I can feel my lats during scapular pulls
- [ ] 5 negatives that each take a real 5 seconds
- [ ] Assistance reduced to near-zero

<!-- FILE: 05-dynamic-pull/02-archer-and-weighted.md -->
Once you own 3×8–10 strict pull-ups, two paths add strength: **load** (weighted) and
**unilateral leverage** (archer → one-arm).

## Weighted pull-ups

- Use a dip belt or hold a dumbbell between the feet.
- **Rep ranges:** 4–6 sets of 3–6 reps for strength. Full dead hang, chin clear, controlled.
- **Progression:** add ~2.5 kg every 1–2 weeks, or add a rep then reset weight up.
- Milestone: a **bodyweight-added pull-up** (e.g. +40 kg at 70 kg BW) is genuinely strong.

## Archer pull-ups → one-arm

1. **Archer pull-up** — pull to one hand while the other arm stays straight along the bar,
   only assisting. Alternate sides. 3×4 each.
2. **Assisted one-arm pull-up** — free hand grips the working wrist or a towel, giving less
   help over time. 3×3 each.
3. **One-arm negative** — from the top, one hand, lower over 5+ s. 3×2 each.
4. **One-arm pull-up** — the standard. Years of consistent work.

## Form cues

- Archer: keep the assisting arm **straight** — if it bends, it's doing too much.
- Expect **elbow and biceps-tendon** load to spike — ramp slowly, warm up thoroughly.
- Brace hard against rotation; the torso wants to twist.

## Self-check

- [ ] Weighted pull-up with +25% bodyweight for 3 reps, **or**
- [ ] 3×4 clean archer pull-ups each side
- [ ] Elbows pain-free with the added load

<!-- FILE: 05-dynamic-pull/03-muscle-up-transition.md -->
The muscle-up is a pull-up, a transition, and a dip stitched together. The **transition**
— getting from "chest at the bar" to "hands pressing down on top" — is what everyone gets
stuck on, and it's trainable in isolation.

## Prerequisites

- ~8–10 strict pull-ups **and** ~8 strict dips (or straight-bar dips).
- Comfortable pulling **explosively** to sternum/nipple height on a bar.

## Transition drills

1. **Straight-bar support + forward roll-over** — from a straight-bar support, slowly lower
   the chest forward toward the bar and back. Learn the top position. 3×5.
2. **Band-assisted transitions** — thick band over the bar, foot in it; pull high, then
   drive the elbows up and over the bar into support. 3×5.
3. **Jumping muscle-ups** — from a box, use a small jump to take you through the sticking
   point; focus on a *fast* transition and a clean catch. 4×3.
4. **Negative muscle-ups** — start in straight-arm support on the bar, lower slowly through
   the transition into a dead hang. 4×2, 4–5 s each. **This is the key exercise.**

## The cue that unlocks it

**"Pull the bar to your hips, then punch your hands down."** As you reach peak pull height,
aggressively lean the chest over the bar and drive the hands from in-front to on-top. Speed
through the transition — hesitation kills it.

## Self-check

- [ ] Explosive pull-ups to sternum height, 3×3
- [ ] 4×2 slow negative muscle-ups through a controlled transition
- [ ] Comfortable and stable in a straight-bar support, leaning forward

<!-- FILE: 05-dynamic-pull/04-muscle-up-clean.md -->
Now assemble the full rep and clean it up — no chicken-wing, no kip, both hands together.

## Building the first rep

Work in this order, dropping assistance as each gets solid:

1. **False grip** (optional on bar, useful on rings) — wrist over the bar so the transition
   distance is shorter. Build false-grip hang time, 3×20 s.
2. **Explosive pull → fast transition → press out.** Do singles with full rest. 5×1.
3. If you catch in a **chicken-wing** (one elbow up first): slow down, do more negatives,
   and cue "both elbows up together."
4. **Kip vs strict:** a small hip drive is fine for your first ever rep, but immediately
   train toward **strict** — dead hang, no leg swing.

## Cleaning it up

- **Both hands transition together.** Film from the front.
- **No excessive lean-back** on the pull — stay under the bar, then explode.
- **Controlled lower** — reverse the whole movement slowly. This builds the strength that
  makes the concentric easy and protects the shoulders.

## Programming

2×/week: 5–8 total quality reps (singles/doubles, long rest) + transition negatives +
straight-bar explosive dips. Deload every 4–6 weeks — the muscle-up is hard on the elbows.

## Progressions from here

Strict muscle-up → **slow muscle-up** → **weighted muscle-up** → **ring muscle-up** →
**wide / L-sit muscle-up**.

## Self-check

- [ ] 1+ strict bar muscle-up, both hands together, no chicken-wing
- [ ] Controlled negative through the transition
- [ ] Can repeat it fresh (a fluke rep doesn't count)

<!-- FILE: 05-dynamic-pull/05-explosive-pulls.md -->
Explosive (plyometric) pulling builds the rate of force development that muscle-ups,
high pull-ups and bar work need — and it's a fun way to test progress.

## The progression

1. **Explosive pull-ups** — pull as high and fast as possible, aiming for chest, then
   sternum, then lower-ribs to the bar. 5×3, full rest.
2. **Chest-to-bar pull-ups** — strict, chest actually contacts the bar. 4×5.
3. **Clapping / release pull-ups** — pull high enough to briefly release the bar (over a
   safe setup). 5×2.
4. **High bar / catch drills** — pull, release, re-catch higher. Advanced, spotter or mats.

## Form cues

- **Full range first** — a fast half-rep is worthless. Dead hang → high as possible.
- **Brace the whole body** — no leg kick. The power comes from the lats and a violent
  scapular pull.
- Keep volume low and quality high; this is nervous-system work, not a burnout finisher.

## Programming

Once a week, early in the session when fresh. 12–18 total explosive reps. Pair with strict
strength work on other days.

## Self-check

- [ ] Strict chest-to-bar pull-ups, 4×5
- [ ] Explosive pull-ups reaching lower-rib/sternum height, 5×3
- [ ] Body stays braced — no kipping — on every rep

<!-- FILE: 06-programming/01-principles.md -->
Movements are only half the course. This module turns them into a plan that keeps working.

## Progressive overload — the four levers

You must make *something* harder over time. In calisthenics the levers are:

1. **Reps / seconds** — +1 rep or +5 s per week.
2. **Leverage** — tuck → advanced tuck → straddle → full.
3. **Load** — weighted pull-ups/dips, +2.5 kg per 1–2 weeks.
4. **Density** — same work in less time (shorter rests), or more sets.

Pick **one lever per exercise per week**. Write down what you changed.

## Intensity vs volume

- **Static skills & explosive work** = high intensity, low volume, long rest, 2×/week.
- **Dynamic strength (pull-ups, dips, push-ups)** = moderate volume, 2–3×/week.
- **Prehab / mobility** = low intensity, high frequency, daily.

## Recovery is the training

Strength is built between sessions. The non-negotiables:

- **Sleep 7–9 h.** The single biggest lever.
- **Protein ~1.6–2.2 g/kg/day.** Enough total calories to support your goal.
- **48 h** between hard sessions for the same movement pattern and joint.
- If a joint (elbow, wrist, shoulder) aches into the next session, **that's the ceiling** —
  reduce volume there for 1–2 weeks.

## Self-check

- [ ] I know which lever I'm progressing on each main exercise
- [ ] Hard sessions for the same pattern are 48 h apart
- [ ] Sleep and protein are actually handled, not just "planned"

<!-- FILE: 06-programming/02-weekly-split.md -->
A template you can run for months. Adjust volume to your recovery, not your ambition.

## 3-day full-body (recommended for most)

**Day A — Pull focus**
- Static pull skill (front/back lever): holds + eccentrics — 20 min
- Pull-ups or weighted pull-ups — 4×5–8
- Row variation — 3×10
- Core: L-sit or hollow work — 3 sets

**Day B — Push focus**
- Static push skill (planche/handstand): leans + tuck holds — 20 min
- Dips or weighted dips — 4×6–10
- Pike / HSPU progression — 3×5–8
- Push-up variation — 2×AMRAP-2

**Day C — Skill + explosive**
- Handstand practice — 15 min
- Explosive pulls — 5×3
- Muscle-up work (if applicable) — singles
- Full-body accessory + prehab

## 4-day upper/lower-ish (more advanced)

Pull / Push / Legs+Core / Skill. Same content, more room per session.

## Rules

- **Skill before strength before conditioning**, always, within a session.
- Warm up the specific joints you're about to load (Module 1, lesson 3).
- Legs matter — 2×/week of pistols/nordics/hamstring work keeps you balanced and injury-resistant.

## Self-check

- [ ] I have a written A/B/C (or 4-day) template
- [ ] Every session starts with skill/technique work while fresh
- [ ] Legs are trained at least twice a week

<!-- FILE: 06-programming/03-deloads-and-peaking.md -->
## Deloads

Every **4–6 weeks**, take a lighter week:

- Cut **volume by ~50%** (halve the sets), keep intensity moderate.
- Drop explosive and max-effort work entirely.
- Keep mobility and easy skill practice.

Signs you needed it: stalled progress, achy tendons, poor sleep, dread before training.
Deloading is not lost time — it's when adaptation catches up.

## Testing / peaking a skill

To max-test a hold or a skill (e.g. attempt your first muscle-up or a full front lever):

1. Deload the week before.
2. Day of: full warm-up, then **3–5 fresh attempts** with long rest, early in the session.
3. If it's not there in 5 attempts, stop — fatigue only builds bad reps. Retest in 1–2 weeks.

## Retest the assessment

Every 6 weeks, redo the 7-test assessment from Module 1. Track it. If most numbers stall
for two blocks running, change a variable: more/less volume, a new progression step, or a
harder look at sleep and food.

## Self-check

- [ ] A deload is scheduled every 4–6 weeks (in the calendar, not "when I feel like it")
- [ ] I test skills fresh, with a deload before, capped at ~5 attempts
- [ ] The 6-week assessment retest is a habit

<!-- FILE: 06-programming/04-injury-proofing.md -->
Calisthenics injuries are almost always **overuse** at the wrist, elbow (medial/"golfer's"
and biceps tendon) or shoulder (front). Prevention is a small daily tax.

## Wrists

- Full wrist-prep routine before every push/handstand session (Module 1, lesson 3).
- **Loaded wrist curls / extensions** — 2×15 each, 2×/week.
- Use **parallettes** when the floor position hurts; build floor tolerance gradually.

## Elbows

- **Ramp straight-arm loads slowly** — levers and planche add elbow stress fast. +10%/week max.
- **Full-range biceps and triceps work** — 2×12 each, 2×/week, especially the lengthened position.
- Separate heavy **bent-arm** (weighted pull-ups) and heavy **straight-arm** (levers) days.
- Early medial-elbow ache: cut straight-arm volume 50% for 2 weeks, add band curls/extensions.

## Shoulders

- **Prone Y-T-W** and **external rotation** — 2×12, 2×/week for the rotator cuff and lower trap.
- **Never train through front-of-shoulder pinching** in dips or german hangs — regress.
- Balance every push with a pull, and pressing volume with **face-pulls / rows**.

## The rule

Mild soreness in muscle = fine. **Sharp or joint-line pain, or pain that worsens session to
session = stop that movement, regress, and address it now.** Small problems caught early
cost a week; ignored, they cost months.

## Self-check

- [ ] Daily wrist prep + 2×/week loaded wrist work
- [ ] Cuff/scap work (Y-T-W, ER) twice a week
- [ ] I have never trained through joint-line pain "to finish the set"

<!-- FILE: 06-programming/05-twelve-week-plan.md -->
A concrete 12-week block. Pick **one static** and **one dynamic** goal, run the 3-day
split, deload weeks 6 and 12.

## Example goals

| Level | Static goal | Dynamic goal |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | 30 s freestanding-ish handstand / 20 s tuck FL | First 3 strict pull-ups + 5 dips |
| Intermediate | 15 s advanced-tuck front lever | First strict muscle-up |
| Advanced | 8 s straddle planche | +30% bodyweight weighted pull-up |

## The block

**Weeks 1–2 — technique & base.** Learn the progression steps, nail form, moderate volume.
Set your working step for each skill.

**Weeks 3–5 — build.** Add one lever (reps/seconds/leverage/load) per exercise per week.
Push the static holds; grind the dynamic strength.

**Week 6 — deload.** Half volume, no max work.

**Weeks 7–9 — intensify.** Harder progression steps, lower reps, more rest. Introduce
explosive work. Start skill-test attempts if close.

**Weeks 10–11 — peak.** Lowest volume, highest intensity. Frequent short skill practice.
Test attempts each week, fresh.

**Week 12 — deload + retest.** Redo the assessment. Log everything. Pick the next block's goals.

## Track this every session

Date · exercise · step/variation · sets×reps or seconds · load · RPE (1–10) · one note.

That log is the most valuable thing you'll build in this course. Re-read it before planning
the next block.

## Self-check

- [ ] I've chosen one static + one dynamic goal for the block
- [ ] Deloads are in the calendar for weeks 6 and 12
- [ ] I have a session log format ready to use
