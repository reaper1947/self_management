The handstand is a static skill *and* a balance skill. You build the strength and shape
against a wall first, then transfer it to freestanding.

## Two wall drills — do both

1. **Chest-to-wall handstand** — walk your feet up the wall until your chest and stomach
   face it and your body is vertical and straight. This trains the correct **line**.
   *Target: 3×45 s, straight body.*
2. **Back-to-wall handstand** — kick up with your back near the wall, only heels touching.
   This lets you feel balance and practice small corrections. *Target: 3×45 s.*

## The line (this is the whole skill)

From hands to toes: **wrists stacked under shoulders under hips under heels**. Ribs down,
posterior pelvic tilt, shoulders fully "open" (pushed up into the ears — the one place you
*want* to shrug), fingers actively gripping the floor.

## Balance basics

- Corrections come from the **fingers and wrists**, not the hips. Falling forward → press
  the fingertips. Falling back → let the heel of the hand take more load.
- Look at the floor between your hands, not forward.

## Progression to freestanding

Once you own 45 s chest-to-wall with a straight line: practice **kick-ups to balance** in
an open space, aiming to find 1–2 seconds of freestanding time. Bail by turning out
(cartwheel) — never collapse straight down.
