A straight bar dip looks like a parallel bar dip and is not one. The bar is in the
way, which forces your torso to lean around it, which turns it into a very
different exercise — and one that maps directly onto the top half of a muscle-up.

## What it is

Supported on a single straight bar, arms locked, torso leaning forward over the
bar. Lower until your chest approaches the bar, then press back up.

## Why it matters

The **transition** of a muscle-up ends exactly here: chest over the bar, elbows
behind, pressing to lockout. If you cannot do a straight bar dip, you cannot
finish a muscle-up, no matter how strong your pull is.

So this is not an optional variant. It is a muscle-up prerequisite that most
people discover far too late.

## The physics

Because the bar sits in front of you, you must lean your torso forward to clear
it. That lean puts your centre of mass well in front of the support — a larger d
than a leaning parallel-bar dip, and the load lands squarely on the **chest and
front delt**.

There is also a stability cost: on one bar your hands cannot move independently,
so any rotation has to be resisted rather than accommodated. That is why it feels
harder than the numbers suggest.

## The progression

| Rung | What to do | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Parallel bar dip** | 3×10 |
| 2 | **Straight bar support hold** — leaning forward | 20 s |
| 3 | **Negative straight bar dip** — 5 s | 3×5 |
| 4 | **Band-assisted** | 3×8 |
| 5 | **Full straight bar dip** | 3×5 |
| 6 | **Full straight bar dip** | 3×8 |
| 7 | **Weighted** | 3×5 at +5 kg |

## Form cues

**Lean over the bar, not away from it.** The chest should finish in front of your
hands.

**Elbows back and close.** Flaring gives the bar nowhere to go.

**Wrists.** Your wrist is in an awkward, extended position here. Do the wrist prep
and keep the grip firm; a loose grip lets the wrist collapse.

**Chest to the bar, not the chin over it.** Same cue as the muscle-up, for exactly
the same reason.

## What goes wrong

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cannot get past halfway | the lean is not deep enough | more support-hold work at rung 2 |
| Wrist pain | position plus load | prep, firmer grip, fewer reps, more sets |
| Rotating to one side | uneven push | slow down; film from the front |
| Chest hits the bar hard | no control at the bottom | negatives at rung 3 |

## Programming it

Twice a week alongside parallel bar dips — one as the main lift, the other as
accessory. If muscle-ups are your goal, straight bar dips are the main lift and
parallel bar dips are the accessory.

## Target

**3 sets of 8 straight bar dips, chest to bar, controlled.** That is the pressing
half of a muscle-up handled.
